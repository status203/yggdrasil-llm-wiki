<!--
A tombstone is a redirect. The content that used to be here has moved, and this page exists so that everything still pointing at the old name arrives somewhere useful rather than nowhere.

Give the destination as a wikilink and say which relation holds:

  moved to / split into / merged into / superseded by

The relation is what tells a reader what to expect when they arrive. "Split into" warns them that only part of what they wanted is at the other end; "merged into" warns them it is now surrounded by other material. Where the content went to more than one page, name them all.

A tombstone is written by the user, by hand, during editorial reorganisation. No script emits one — `<program>/.yg/bin/move-page.sh` moves a page and repoints its links instead, which is the right answer when every reference can be found and rewritten. A tombstone is for when they cannot: an outside bookmark, a link in a note that lives elsewhere, or a name a reader will simply try again.

Don't keep a copy of the old content here "for reference". A tombstone that holds content is a second copy that will drift from the real page, and a reader who finds it has no way to tell which one is current.
